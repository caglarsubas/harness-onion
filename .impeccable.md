## Design Context

### Users

The primary users are enterprise tenant administrators, platform engineers,
governance and security reviewers, and operations owners assessing one tenant
organization's harness ecosystem. Authorized platform operators additionally
need a cross-organization portfolio view. They use the product to understand
what was requested, what is installed, what is healthy, what is proven, what is
stale or blocked, and where action is required without collapsing those facts
into a misleading single status.

### Brand Personality

Assured, precise, composed. The interface should reduce uncertainty, distinguish
fact from inference, and feel credible during operational and governance review.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a refined enterprise systems-map aesthetic derived from the four-plane,
sixteen-harness onion reference. The onion is the memorable desktop overview and
supports direct plane and harness navigation, but an adjacent semantic status
list is always available. Compact screens use the list as the primary surface.
Use light and dark themes with locally bundled assets and fonts; never depend on
a CDN, hosted analytics, remote font service, or vendor-specific frontend host.

### Design Principles

1. Preserve evidence axes: desired, artifact, deployment, runtime, security,
   assurance, and tenant acceptance remain visibly distinct.
2. Progressive disclosure: organization, plane, then harness detail, with stable
   URLs and breadcrumbs at every level.
3. Accessible equivalence: every visual relationship and interaction in the
   onion has a keyboard-operable, screen-reader-readable list equivalent.
4. Honest freshness: stale, missing, unavailable, not selected, degraded, and
   failed states are explicit and never converted into a healthy aggregate.
5. Tenant safety: organization context is derived from authenticated policy;
   cross-organization access exists only for a separately authorized platform
   operator role and is always audited.
